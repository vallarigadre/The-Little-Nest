December with a toddler can feel like two seasons at once. There is the sparkle: the lights, the music, the excitement on their face when they spot a tree. And there is the reality: short days, cold afternoons, a busy calendar and a little person who wants to touch every single ornament.

This list is for the second part. Here are 25 simple Christmas activities for toddlers that take very little setup, use things you mostly have at home and fit into the small pockets of a real day. None of them need to look perfect. Pick one, set it out, and let your toddler lead.

## How to use this list (without making December a project)

Think of these ideas as a menu, not a checklist. You don't need to do all 25, and you definitely don't need to do one every day.

1. **Pick three for the week.** One sensory idea, one making idea and one movement game is a lovely, balanced week.
2. **Set it out before they wake or after nap.** A tray on a low table is an invitation. Toddlers love to discover something new waiting for them.
3. **Keep it short.** Ten or fifteen minutes is a full activity at this age. If they wander off after five, that counts too.
4. **Repeat the favorites.** Toddlers learn through repetition. The jingle bell bottle on day one and day ten is a different experience for them.

A quick safety note before we start: a few of these ideas use small items like jingle bells or pinecone pieces. For children under 3, keep small parts sealed away or skip them, and stay close by for anything that could end up in a mouth.

## Sensory Christmas activities

Sensory play is the easiest way into the season for the youngest toddlers. It calms busy bodies and asks very little of you.

1. **Jingle bell sensory bottle.** Fill a clear plastic bottle with water or rice, add a few jingle bells and a pinch of glitter, then glue the lid shut and tape it. Shake, roll and listen. A pack of [Arrowbash empty sensory bottles](https://link.amazon/B07uDsOUm) makes it easy to make a few at once, but a washed water bottle works too. Because the bells are a choking hazard, check the lid is sealed every time.
2. **Evergreen sensory bin.** Add a few pine sprigs, large pinecones, wooden spoons and a couple of cups to a shallow bin. Let your toddler scoop, sniff and fill. Supervise closely with under 3s, since pinecones can break into small pieces.
3. **Christmas play dough.** Store-bought or homemade dough plus a few holiday cookie cutters keeps little hands busy for ages. A [Christmas cookie cutter baking set for kids](https://link.amazon/B0cRuLrcy) with a small rolling pin is sized for toddler hands. Homemade dough is very salty, so it's for squishing, not snacking.
4. **Frozen ornament rescue.** Freeze a few shatterproof ornaments or wooden shapes in a container of water overnight. Give your toddler a tray, a cup of warm water and a spoon to melt them free. Put a towel underneath and stay close.
5. **Crinkle paper basket.** Fill a basket with old wrapping paper and tissue. Babies and young toddlers love to crumple, tear and toss it. This is a perfect activity for the youngest ones, and it costs nothing.

## Busy hands: fine motor Christmas play

These ideas strengthen little fingers for later skills like holding a crayon, and they are calm enough for a pre-dinner wind down.

6. **Sticky wall Christmas tree.** Tape a tree shape of [clear contact paper](https://link.amazon/B0ev9SWgj) to the wall at toddler height, sticky side out. Offer paper stars, felt circles or tissue squares to stick on, peel off and stick on again.
7. **Unwrapping practice box.** Wrap a few blocks, board books or toy animals loosely in paper with just one piece of tape each. Unwrapping is real work for a toddler, and it makes Christmas morning less overwhelming.
8. **Ornament color sort.** Set out a handful of large shatterproof ornaments and two or three bowls. Sort by color, or simply move them from one bowl to another. Skip glass ornaments and metal hooks.
9. **Painter's tape tree peel.** Make a tree shape on the floor or a tray with strips of painter's tape. Toddlers peel the strips off one by one. It's surprisingly absorbing.
10. **Posting old Christmas cards.** Cut a slot in a shoebox lid and let your toddler post last year's cards through it. Older toddlers can name the pictures as they go.

## Simple Christmas crafts toddlers can actually do

Toddler crafts are about the process, not the result. Expect wobbly, messy and wonderful.

11. **Dot marker Christmas tree.** Draw a simple tree outline and let your toddler fill it with dots. [Do A Dot Art! washable dot markers](https://link.amazon/B09AjMfkT) are chunky enough for small fists and less messy than paint.
12. **Torn paper wreath.** Cut the middle out of a paper plate, brush it with glue and let your toddler stick on torn green paper. Tearing is great for hand strength.
13. **Stamped wrapping paper.** Roll out a length of [brown kraft paper](https://link.amazon/B0eiD0N4g), add washable paint and let your toddler stamp shapes all over it. A set of [YGDZ paint sponges for kids](https://link.amazon/B0gNxk3tv) includes easy-grip stampers. Use it to wrap gifts for grandparents.
14. **Salt dough ornaments.** Mix one cup of flour, half a cup of salt and about half a cup of water, roll it out, press in a handprint or cut a star, and bake low until hard. Your toddler can paint them the next day. Salt dough is not food, so keep an eye on tasting.
15. **Painted pinecones.** Dab white or green paint on large pinecones for a snowy look, then line them up on a windowsill. Great for 2 and 3 year olds with a smock on.

## Christmas movement games for wiggly days

When the weather keeps you inside and the energy is high, these games help.

16. **Reindeer hop.** Put on some music and hop like reindeer, gallop like Santa's horses, waddle like penguins and tiptoe like elves. Take turns choosing the animal.
17. **Jingle bell freeze dance.** Play Christmas songs, shake bells while the music plays and freeze when it stops. Toddlers find this endlessly funny.
18. **Indoor snowball toss.** A set of [Fun Express plush snowballs](https://link.amazon/B0fquOekn) turns any afternoon into a soft, giggly snowball game. Toss them into a laundry basket or have a gentle family snowball fight.
19. **Present delivery route.** Fill a few empty boxes, give your toddler a wagon, a basket or a toy truck, and let them deliver "presents" to each room. They love having an important job.
20. **Pinecone nature walk.** Bundle up, take a basket and go looking for pinecones, sticks and evergreen sprigs. Bring them home for the sensory bin or for painting.

## Cozy, quiet Christmas activities

Some of the best moments of the season are slow ones. These are perfect for the late afternoon or the hour before bed.

21. **Book picnic by the tree.** Spread a blanket by the tree, switch on the lights and read together. A touchy-feely book like [That's Not My Reindeer](https://link.amazon/B0i7Jc5dJ) is perfect for little hands that need to touch everything.
22. **A tree just for them.** Give your toddler a small tabletop tree or a branch in a jar with a few unbreakable ornaments to hang, take down and hang again. It saves the big tree, too.
23. **Ornament washing station.** Set up a tub of warm soapy water, a cloth and some plastic ornaments or toy dishes. Washing is a calming practical life activity that toddlers adore.
24. **Window light watching.** After dark, sit by a window and look for lights, count the colors and name them together. It's lovely when the day has been long.
25. **Five calm minutes at the table.** A simple printed page to trace, match or count is a gentle way to sit together while dinner cooks.

## Christmas activities by age

Every toddler is different, so treat these as loose starting points.

**12 to 18 months.** Keep it sensory and simple: the crinkle paper basket, the jingle bell bottle (sealed), unwrapping practice, the reindeer hop and the book picnic. Expect short bursts of attention and lots of mouthing, so skip small parts entirely.

**2 year olds.** Most of this list works well. Try the sticky wall tree, painter's tape peel, dot marker tree, ornament sort, play dough and the snowball toss. Two year olds love real jobs, so the present delivery route and the washing station are often big hits.

**3 year olds.** Add more steps and a little more independence: salt dough ornaments from mixing to painting, stamped wrapping paper for family gifts, sorting by two colors at once and counting snowballs into the basket. Many 3 year olds are ready to sit for a few printed tracing or matching pages, too.
