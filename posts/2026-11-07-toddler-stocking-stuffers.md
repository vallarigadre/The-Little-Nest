There's a moment every Christmas morning that so many of us know well. The stocking comes down, the little treasures tumble out, and ten minutes later half of them are under the couch, broken, or quietly heading for the trash. Tiny plastic trinkets are cheap and easy to grab at the checkout, but they rarely last until New Year's.

The good news is that a toddler's stocking doesn't need to be full of stuff to feel full of magic. Toddlers love small, real things they can hold, shake, open and use. A board book with flaps. A silk scarf that floats. A wooden animal that fits perfectly in one hand.

Below you'll find a simple formula for filling a stocking with less, my favorite non-plastic stocking stuffers sorted by age, a few free ideas from around the house, and a quick safety check, because the small size that makes something a stocking stuffer is also what can make it risky for little ones.

## A simple stocking formula: read, play, make, use

You may know the "something you want, something you need, something to wear, something to read" rhyme for gifts. Here's a toddler-sized version for the stocking. Pick one from each, and you're done:

1. **Something to read.** A small board book, ideally a holiday one you can read together on Christmas morning and pack away with the decorations.
2. **Something to play.** One open-ended toy that can be used in lots of ways, like a play scarf, a wooden animal or a simple instrument.
3. **Something to make.** A tiny art supply that invites them to create, like chunky crayons or a lacing toy for older toddlers.
4. **Something to use.** A practical, everyday thing made special: a new toothbrush, cozy socks or their own little cup.

That's four things. It looks lovely in a stocking, it's easy on the budget, and almost everything gets used well into the new year. If grandparents ask what to buy, this list also makes a gentle, easy answer.

## Stocking stuffers for 2 year olds

Two year olds are busy little people. They're starting to pretend, they love to name and sort things, and many are ready to make their first real marks with a crayon.

Use them for animal sounds, a little Noah's ark in a shoebox, or a "zoo" made of blocks. Check the age label for your child.
- **Chunky beeswax crayons.** [Stockmar beeswax block crayons in a tin](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0007QJ7ZG?tag=creativedi03c-20) are flat blocks that are easy for small fists to hold, and they make lovely wide strokes of color. - **Cozy socks with grips.** Something to wear is always useful, and a pair of grippy socks in a soft, plain color will get worn all winter.

## Stocking stuffers for 3 year olds

Three year olds can focus for longer, love "big kid" jobs and enjoy a little challenge. This is a great age for stocking stuffers that build fine motor skills.

- **A lacing toy.** The [Kizh wooden lacing apple](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07DRJ7957?tag=creativedi03c-20) has a little wooden worm on a string that winds in and out of the apple. It's quiet, compact and perfect for car rides and restaurants. Because it has a string, keep it for supervised play and store it out of reach of younger siblings.
- **Their own crayons and a small notebook.** The beeswax block crayons above work beautifully at this age too. Add a plain notebook and you have a whole morning's worth of drawing.
- **A real tool, child sized.** Three year olds love to help. A small dustpan and brush, a child-sized watering can or their own wooden spoon for baking can feel more exciting than any toy, because it means they get to join in.
- **A printable activity book.** A few pages of tracing, matching and counting tucked into the stocking makes a calm, screen-free start to the day while the grown-ups make breakfast. (More on that below.)

## A quick stocking safety check

Small gifts and small children need a little extra care. Before you fill the stocking, run through this list. It only takes a minute:

- **Use the toilet paper roll test.** If an item, or any part that could come off, fits inside an empty toilet paper roll, it's a choking hazard for children under 3. That includes many small toys, beads, marbles, pom poms and party favors.
- **Skip button batteries and magnets.** Tiny batteries (in light-up toys, musical cards and some remote-control toys) and small strong magnets are very dangerous if swallowed. If you have any of these in the house, keep them well out of reach.
- **Avoid balloons for under 8s.** Uninflated or popped balloon pieces are a serious choking risk.
- **Watch for strings and ribbons.** Long cords, ribbons and drawstrings can be a strangulation risk. Trim curling ribbon off wrapping and supervise any toy with a string.
- **Read every age label.** It's there for safety, not just for skill level. A toy marked 3+ is not meant for a younger sibling, even if they're "advanced."
- **Be careful with candy.** Hard candies, mini candy canes, gum, nuts and popcorn are choking hazards for toddlers. Leave them out of little stockings and keep older siblings' treats out of reach.

If you're unsure about a specific item, your pediatrician is always a good person to ask.

## Free and almost-free stocking fillers

Some of the most loved stocking stuffers cost nothing at all. A few gentle ideas:

- **A library book, wrapped.** Borrow a Christmas book or two in December and wrap them. Returning them afterward is part of the fun.
- **A "ticket" for an outing.** Draw a simple picture on a card: a walk to see Christmas lights, a hot cocoa date, or a trip to feed the ducks.
- **A favorite family photo.** Print one small photo of grandparents, cousins or the family pet for them to carry around and talk about.
- **Nature finds.** A big pine cone, a smooth stone too large to swallow, or a sprig of rosemary to smell. Toddlers adore these.
- **Something from your kitchen.** A small whisk, a measuring cup or a wooden spoon, wrapped in tissue, can become their very own "baking tool."
