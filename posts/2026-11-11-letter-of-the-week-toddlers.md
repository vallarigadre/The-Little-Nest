"Letter of the week" is one of the most loved tot school ideas, and for good reason. It gives your week a simple theme, it is easy to plan, and toddlers love seeing "their" letter pop up everywhere.

The key for little ones is keeping it playful. At 2 and 3, the goal is not reading. It is noticing letters, hearing their sounds, and connecting them to things your toddler already loves, like apples, balls and cats.

Here is a gentle routine you can use for any letter, plus simple activities that take ten minutes or less.

## Is my toddler ready?

Most toddlers start showing interest in letters somewhere between 2 and 3, often when they notice the first letter of their own name. If your toddler likes pointing at signs, books or cereal boxes, they are ready to play with letters.

If they are not interested yet, that is fine too. Keep reading together and try again in a few months. Letter of the week works best when it feels like a game, not a lesson.

## Which order to go in

There is no single right order. A few easy options:

- **Start with their name.** The first letter of your toddler's name is the most meaningful letter they know.
- **Go in alphabet order.** Simple and easy to remember, and it takes about half a year.
- **Start with easy sounds.** Letters like M, S, B and A have clear sounds toddlers can hear and copy.

Pick whichever feels easiest for you. Consistency matters more than the order.

## A simple five-day routine

Use the same rhythm every week so you never have to wonder what is next. Each day takes about ten minutes.

1. **Monday: meet the letter.** Show the letter, say its name and its sound, and trace it with your finger together. Read a favorite alphabet book and find the letter on the page.
2. **Tuesday: letter hunt.** Hide three or four things that start with the letter around the room (for B: a ball, a banana, a book, a bear). Find them together and say each word.
3. **Wednesday: hands on the letter.** Fill a big outline of the letter with dot stickers, dot markers or torn paper.
4. **Thursday: build it.** Make the letter with play dough snakes, wooden blocks or yarn.
5. **Friday: sort and celebrate.** Sort a few objects into "starts with our letter" and "does not." Then add the letter to a wall or binder of letters you have learned.

## Keep it gentle

- **Follow their interest.** If your toddler loves trucks, T can last two weeks.
- **Say the sound more than the name.** "B says buh" helps more later than "this is B."
- **Never quiz.** Name things together instead of asking "What letter is this?"
- **Mind the small parts.** Keep small letter pieces and stickers for supervised play only, especially with children under 3.
