## What makes a toy worth keeping

When I think about a gift that lasts, I look for three things:

- **It does less, so your toddler does more.** A drum waits to be tapped. A fire station waits for a story. Toys that don't perform on their own invite longer, deeper play.
- **It grows with them.** The best gifts work one way at 2 and a whole new way at 3.
- **It's sturdy and beautiful enough to keep.** Wood, soft fabric and simple design age well, on the shelf and in your heart.

## How each toy supports play at home

A beautiful toy is only half the gift. The other half is a little space and time to use it. Here's how these toys fit into everyday play:

- **Movement (walker, piano mat):** Big body play helps toddlers burn energy indoors, which is a gift in itself during winter. Clear a small open space and let them go.
- **Music (pound and tap bench, jungle drum set):** Rhythm games build listening and turn-taking. Try "I play, you play" and keep it silly.
- **Puzzles and stacking (alphabet board, fruit stacker):** These build focus and problem solving. Offer them on a tray at a low table, and stay close the first few times.
- **Caring play (baby dolls, plush puppy):** Nurturing play lets toddlers practice gentleness and act out their own day: meals, baths, naps.
- **Storytelling (fire station):** Small worlds help 3 year olds tell stories, try out feelings and play side by side with a sibling or friend.

A simple tip: don't put every new gift out on Christmas Day. Choose one or two, tuck the rest away, and rotate them into your toy shelf in January. Each one feels new all over again.
